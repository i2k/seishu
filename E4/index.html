Reconstruction from the Earthquake

THE NEW ZEALANDERS 

　At the Library Exhibition in 1933, "Reconstruction from the Earthquake," donated books from the British government were displayed in the exhibition hall, "Chinretsu-shitsu" on the second floor. According to the catalog, two or three books related to natural science were selected for the exhibition.
 　A large-sized book, THE NEW ZEALANDERS (A100:1038) is one of the valuable books in the library. This book was donated from the government of the United Kingdom to the University of Tokyo (at that time, Tokyo Imperial University), after the Great Kanto Earthquake, as "a token of sympathy and abiding friendship."
　The author, Angus (1822-1886), was a painter, natural scientist and explorer. The book depicts the lives and culture of the people  in New Zealand through colorful lithographs.（「文化資源としての書物」履修生）

     Kujira-no-zu

　"Kujira-no-zu" (T86:108) is an accordion-style book depicting whales. It has dimensions of 27.3cm in height, 25.6cm in width, and when fully unfolded, it measures 10 meters and 20 centimeters in length. It is one of the collections of Yoshio Tanaka (1838-1916), a naturalist during the Meiji period.
　Whale hunting has been practiced in Japan since ancient times, and various parts of the whale, such as oil and bones, had practical uses. The book describes detailed information includes the small eyes of the whales and their ability to spout water.
　At the end of the book, it remains Tanaka's handwritten note (written in the 25th year of the Meiji era, 1892).  It is mentioned that Tanaka purchased  "Kujira-no-zu" in Osaka and rebound it in accordion format for easier reading. The year and place of original production are unknown.

Chūfu

　In the second-floor exhibition room, books donated  from the Tanaka baron family were displayed. According to the catalog, approximately 10,000 books from the collection of  Yoshio Tanaka were donated by Mitsuo Tanaka.
　One of the donated books is "Chūfu" (original illustrations by Jakusōan Yoshida). The book depicts small insects in actual size (Chū means the insect in Japanese).
　Jakusōan Yoshida (1805-1859) was a botanist during the late Edo period."Chūfu" consists of a total of 11 volumes, with dimensions of 286mm in height and 207mm in width. Yoshio Tanaka's handwritten note (dated February of the 23rd year of Meiji, 1890) remains, stating that he rebound the 11 volumes into 5 volumes. In the late Showa period, they were combined into a single volume, and currently, they are bound in a Western-style cloth binding.

Hyakkunin-ikku

　The Chikurei Bunko is a special library collection  that belonged to Chikurei Tsunoda (1857-1919) . About 1500 books of Chikurei Bunko were donated after the Great Kanto Earthquake.  Hyakunin-ikku is a compilation of poems edited by Fukaku Tachiba (1662-1753), a haiku master of the mid-Edo period, and transcribed into manuscripts by Gesshin Saito (1804-1878) during the Tenpo era (1830-1844) .
　Currently, it has been digitized by the National Institute of Japanese Literature's "Historical Texts NW Project."
